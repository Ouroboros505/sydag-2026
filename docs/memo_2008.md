# Memo: which lines to test in 2008

**To:** Head of the maize pipeline (115-day, first-stage testcrosses) · **From:** Team ProMaize · **January 2008**

## The decision

We have plots for about 30% of the 15,962 new lines (4,790 lines at about seven locations each). A
line we do not test this year can never be advanced. None of the 157 families behind these lines has
been in a field before.

## What we recommend

**Test the same share of every family, and let the DNA choose which brothers and sisters get the
plots.** The list is ready: `docs/advance_2008.csv`, 4,789 lines, every family represented.

## Why

1. **Every family is new, so a family's average can only be guessed from its parents.** That guess is
   good when the parents have been tested before and weak when they have not.
2. **This year the parents are mostly unknown.** 24 of the 157 families have neither parent tested
   before, and 88 more have only one. Two earlier years looked like this: 2003 and 2007.
3. **In those years, spreading the plots evenly cost little**: $2.02 and 33 cents an acre compared
   with betting on the best-looking families, against $4.50 to $7.50 in years with well-known
   parents, and it kept about twice as many families alive. We knew that in January. (Checked
   afterwards: in 2008 it cost 8 cents an acre, again for twice the families.)
4. **Inside a family, the DNA ranking works every year.** It reliably tells which siblings are better,
   even when it cannot tell which family is better. The even split uses exactly that part.

## The two options

| | Conservative (recommended) | Aggressive |
|---|---|---|
| What it does | same share of plots to every family, DNA picks the siblings | every line ranked by predicted $/acre |
| Families kept (2008 lines) | 157, about 138 "effective" | 132, about 71 "effective" |
| Value in the 2008 field | $5.0 per acre over random picks | $5.1 per acre over random picks |
| Average over six past seasons | $5.5 per acre | $8.8 per acre |
| Best when | the parents are new (2003, 2007, 2008) | the parents have a long record (2004-2006) |
| Risk | leaves a little value when the family guess is good | concentrates plots on guesses we cannot yet trust |

In 2008 both options clearly beat the standard genomic method, which realised $1.2 per acre; over
six seasons the aggressive option averaged $8.8 against its $5.5. With the standard method we would
have needed 1,277 more lines in 2008 (about 8,900 plots) to find as many of the season's real winners.

## How far to trust this

- We checked it the only fair way: predict each past season from the seasons before it, then compare
  with what the field did. Six seasons, 2003 to 2008; it beat the standard method in all six.
- It is a first screen, not a final answer: field plots are noisy (a line's yield swings more from
  field to field than lines differ from each other), so no prediction can be close to perfect.
- Every line comes with a range it will probably land in. In 2008, 90% of lines landed in theirs.
- We predict the line's value across all locations, not location by location: we tested location
  by location and it could not be predicted, because each line is tested in one year only.

## What we need from you

1. The real plot budget, if it is not 30%.
2. Your drying cost, moisture target and harvest loss from lodging, so the dollars are your dollars.
3. Confirmation that at this stage lines are judged on how good a parent they are (their effect
   across testers), not on the one tester they were crossed to.

The interactive version, with every number and both options, is at **promaize.stipe.app**.
